Why the Ford Bronco Roll Cage Needs These Four Upgrades

The Bronco's roll cage is high-strength steel permanently welded into the body. You cannot add or remove the cage itself. What you can do is use it effectively. The cage bars run along the A-pillars, B-pillars, and across the roofline in precise, fixed positions. Aftermarket brands have designed bolt-on accessories specifically around those positions, using existing hardware points without drilling or permanent modification to the Bronco's structure.

The four main upgrade categories for the Bronco roll cage are:

  • Grab handles: Paracord-wrapped aftermarket handles mount to roll cage bars and replace factory plastic loops with something that actually grips during steep descents, off-camber traverses, or any technical section where a passenger needs a secure hold.
  • Overhead MOLLE storage panels: Mount to the cage bars above the rear seats and create an attachment grid for bags, radios, first aid kits, and trail tools without consuming floor or cargo space.
  • Roll bar padding: Wraps exposed cage tubing at head height. The Bronco cabin puts bars in positions where head strikes occur during rollovers or hard off-road contact. Padding is a meaningful safety upgrade for any trail Bronco.
  • Chase racks and sport bar mounts: Attach to the rear sport bars of the cage, providing a rear-mounted rack for Hi-Lift jacks, spare tires, shovels, and recovery gear that stays secure at speed on rough terrain.

Together, these four upgrades transform a bare-cage Bronco interior into a functional off-road command center. Here are the best products in each category, verified for image accuracy and pricing.

How to Build a Complete Ford Bronco Roll Cage Interior System

The most effective Bronco cage builds treat these accessories as a system rather than individual purchases. Here is the sequence that makes the most practical sense for a trail and overland focused Bronco:

  • Step 1 — Safety first: Roll bar padding. Wrap the A-pillar cage bars and any exposed tubing at head height with fire-retardant foam. This is the lowest cost and highest safety impact upgrade on the list. Do this before anything else.
  • Step 2 — Grab handles. Replace or supplement the factory plastic loops with paracord grab handles. Install them at every door position for 4-door Broncos. Trail passengers will thank you on the first rough section.
  • Step 3 — Seat covers. Bartact tactical seat covers add the MOLLE seatback panel that lets the cage interior become one integrated gear management system. Once the seats are upgraded, the overhead panels and cage storage all connect to the same organizational philosophy.
  • Step 4 — Overhead MOLLE panel. With seats done, add the overhead storage above the rear row. This is where first aid, communications, navigation, and frequently accessed trail gear lives. It is accessible from both front and rear and keeps that gear off the floor.
  • Step 5 — Chase rack (if overlanding). For Broncos that run remote trails requiring self-rescue capability, add the sport bar chase rack for Hi-Lift, recovery boards, and a full-size spare. This is the heaviest and most visible upgrade; do it last after the interior is dialed in.

A fully built roll cage Bronco interior using this sequence costs a fraction of an aftermarket suspension or bumper build, but delivers dramatically improved day-to-day usability and genuine safety improvements for trail driving. The cage was designed to be used. Use it.
